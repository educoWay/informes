Research Universities vs Applied Science

In most European countries there are two types of universities that are important to know and differentiate: universities of Research and those of Applied Sciences. The two types of university share many commonalities, but to fully understand these two systems, it is helpful to look at their main differences:

● Theoretical vs. practical methodology

Applied Sciences universities are known for using a highly practical methodology called PBL (Project/Problem-Based Learning). From the first year, students simulate a real-world project-based work environment, collaborating with peers, researching to find and evaluate solutions, and reaching a consensus on a strategy that must then be implemented collectively. It's a fascinating methodology (the same one used in Denmark) that greatly facilitates learning and the acquisition of practical skills that you can apply as soon as you graduate. These universities almost always include a mandatory one-semester professional internship. Research universities don't have such a practical focus. Instead, they aim to establish a solid foundation in theory and research skills so that students can later choose a master's program in which to specialize and acquire more practical skills (more applicable to their future careers). These universities typically don't include internships as part of their undergraduate degree.

● Duration

The universities of Applied Sciences last one year longer (4 years, 240 ECTS) because they include internships and are designed to allow you to enter the job market directly without going through a master's degree. On the other hand, the ones of Research only last 3 years (180 ECTS) because they do not have internships and are usually complemented with a 1 or 2 year master's degree.

● Ranking / Prestige

The main reason why one might choose a university in Research is because of the higher ranking and prestige that these universities have.

The universities of Applied Sciences (University of Applied Sciences, in Dutch) are sometimes undervalued among students because they do not appear in international rankings (they do not have research faculties that develop studies or scientific publications, which is what scores the most in the rankings).

● Access to Master's Degrees

Here it's important to distinguish whether your master's degree will be in a country that differentiates between the two universities, or in a country that doesn't. In Spain, for example, which doesn't differentiate between these universities, both degrees give you the same opportunities. But if it's in the Netherlands, Belgium, or Germany, for example, there are differences. With your degree from the university of Research you will be able to access master's degrees at universities in Research or Applied Sciences. But instead, with your title of Applied Sciences you will be able to access master's degrees at universities in Applied Sciences. But if you want to access universities in Research, you will be required to complete a pre-master's course (lasting either one semester or one year) to bring you up to speed with research skills that you are not expected to have developed yet.

BSc (Bachelor of Science) vs. BEng (Bachelor of Engineering) — Denmark

BSc: more scientific, mathematical, and theoretical orientation. It's a good option for students who enjoy theory and analysis, want to keep open the possibility of doing a master's degree or dedicating themselves to research, and prefer to understand scientific principles in depth.

BEng: a more practical, technical and industry-related approach. It's a good option for students who prefer to learn in a practical way, want to work in engineering from an applied perspective, and are interested in projects, design and development of solutions.
